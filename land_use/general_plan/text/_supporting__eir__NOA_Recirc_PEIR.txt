NOTICE IS HEREBY GIVEN that on December 18, 2025, the City of Oceanside (City), acting as Lead 
Agency, issued for public review and comment a Draft Recirculated Program Environmental Impact 
Report (RPEIR) that evaluates potential environmental effects associated with the proposed City of 
Oceanside General Plan Update (GPU), Smart and Sustainable Corridors Specific Plan (SSCSP) and 
Climate Action Plan (CAP) Update project (project). Pursuant to California Environmental Quality Act 
(CEQA) Guidelines Section 15088.5, a lead agency is required to recirculate an EIR when significant new 
information is added to the EIR after public notice is given of the availability of the draft EIR for public review 
under CEQA Guidelines Section 15087 but before certification. The Onward Oceanside Draft EIR was 
previously circulated for public review from June 4, 2024, to August 5, 2024 (an extended 62-day review 
period). All interested people and organizations had an opportunity during this time to submit their written 
comments on the PEIR to the City. In response to comments received during the public review period, the 
City determined that significant new information would be required to be added to the PEIR, triggering the 
requirement for recirculation. A Readers Guide is available to assist in assessing changes to the Draft 
RPEIR compared to the Draf PEIR which was previously published for public review. The Draft RPEIR may 
be accessed on the City’s website at https://www.ci.oceanside.ca.us/government/development-
services/planning/environmental-documents-ceqa. 
A copy of the Draft RPEIR will be available for public review at the Oceanside Development Services 
Department (300 North Coast Highway, Oceanside, CA 92054), the City of Oceanside Public Library 
(330 North Coast Highway, Oceanside, CA 92054) and the City of Oceanside Mission Branch Public Library 
(3861-B Mission Avenue, Oceanside, CA 92058).  
Public Review and Comment Period: December 18, 2025, through February 1, 2026 
In accordance with state law, a 45-day public review period has been established for the Draft RPEIR. 
Following release of the Draft RPEIR, the City will accept and consider written comments during the public 
review period. Under CEQA, the City is required to respond only to comments that raise substantive issues 
regarding the adequacy of the Draft RPEIR’s environmental analysis. The Draft RPEIR evaluates the 
environmental effects of the proposed project as contemplated by and consistent with the GPU, SSCSP, 
and CAP Update, which establishes the applicable land use framework, development standards, and policy 
assumptions for the project. To receive a formal written response under CEQA, a comment must: 
• 
Address a specific physical environmental impact, mitigation measure, or alternative analyzed in 
the Draft EIR; 
• 
Identify a potential deficiency, inaccuracy, or omission in the environmental analysis; or 


• 
Provide new information related to a physical environmental impact that was not previously 
considered and that could reasonably affect the EIR’s conclusions. 
Comments that do not meet these criteria, including comments related solely to policy content, planning 
assumptions, land use designations, or development standards established by the GPU or SSCSP, or 
comments expressing general support or opposition to those documents or the project do not require a 
formal response under CEQA. Responses to comments will be provided in accordance with CEQA 
Guidelines Section 15088 and will be proportional and focused on environmental issues. Responses may 
clarify or reference existing analysis, explain why a comment does not raise a new or significant 
environmental issue, or explain why no additional analysis or revision to the Draft RPEIR is required. CEQA 
does not require the Lead Agency to resolve policy disagreements or revisit previously adopted planning 
decisions reflected in the GPU or Specific Plan. 
Additionally, only comments submitted during this public review period will be reviewed by the City. 
Comments submitted on the previously circulated Draft PEIR must be resubmitted to be considered. 
All written comments on the adequacy of the Draft RPEIR must be received by the City no later than 
February 1, 2026.  
Written comments should be submitted via e-mail to gpu_2_deir@oceansideca.org or via postal mail to 
Darlene Nicandro, Development Services Director, City of Oceanside, 300 North Coast Highway, 
Oceanside, CA 92054. Following the close of the public review period, responses to comments on the Draft

Project Location:  
The project area consists of the City located in northwestern San Diego County, California. The City is 
bounded by the Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton to the north, unincorporated county of San Diego to 
the east, the city of Vista to the southeast, the city of Carlsbad to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the 
west. The total City area is approximately 42 square miles. 
Project Description:  
The project involves the preparation of six new/updated General Plan elements (GPU), the SSCSP, and 
an update to the City’s 2019 CAP (CAP Update).  
The project constitutes the second phase of a comprehensive GPU, the first phase of which involved the 
preparation of an Economic Development Element, an Energy and Climate Action Element, and the City’s 
first CAP.  These components of the GPU were adopted in 2019. The second phase of the GPU includes 
six new/updated General Plan elements organized around themes and guiding principles that reflect 
community input, the findings of technical studies, and state and regional priorities. These new/updated 
elements address all state-mandated general plan topic areas: land use, transportation, conservation, open 
space, military readiness activities, safety, and noise.  These elements also address the topics of 
environmental justice, community facilities, and hazardous waste. Together, these new/updated elements 
establish new goals, policies, and implementation actions for the physical development of the City. The 
new/updated General Plan elements include: Efficient and Compatible Land Use (ECLU), Integrated 


Mobility (IM), Remarkable Communities (RC), Vital and Sustainable Resources (VSR), Safe and Resilient 
Environment (SRE), and Healthy and Livable Community (HLC).  
Implementation of the second phase of the GPU will involve a wide range of actions that promote 
sustainable and equitable growth and change. These actions include new land use designations and 
policies, urban design standards, capital improvements, new and expanded mobility options, aesthetic 
upgrades, improvements to parks and recreation facilities, natural hazard adaptation strategies, and 
policies that promote the preservation of natural resources and farmland.  
The SSCSP is a regulatory document, consistent with the goals and policies of the ECLU, that outlines 
strategies, standards, and processes meant to foster the revitalization of the Mission Avenue, Oceanside 
Boulevard, and Vista Way commercial corridors. The proposed land use framework for the SSCSP Planning 
Area offers an array of housing options for people at all income levels and stages of life. The non-residential 
land uses are framed to support an inclusive commercial environment that provides retail and services 
catering to residents’ daily needs while simultaneously inviting visitors from across the region.  Additionally, 
the SSCSP seeks to facilitate “complete streets” improvements within the corridors that accommodate all 
modes of transportation. 
The project includes an update to the 2019 CAP to account for the increased housing and employment 
growth resulting from the GPU and SSCSP. The CAP Update will provide a comprehensive plan for 
addressing Greenhouse Gas emissions throughout the City. 
Potential Environmental Effects: 
The Draft RPEIR identifies that the project would result in less than significant impacts (with or without 
mitigation measures) to the following environmental issue areas: land use, aesthetics, agricultural 
resources, biological resources, cultural resources, energy, geology and soils, hazards and hazardous 
materials, hydrology and water quality, mineral resources, population and housing, transportation, and 
wildfire. The Draft RPEIR identifies that the project would result in significant and unavoidable impacts to 
air quality, greenhouse gas emissions, noise, public services, and utilities. For these issue areas, the Draft 
RPEIR concludes that there are no feasible mitigation measures that could reduce potentially significant 
impacts to a less than significant level at the program level of review.  
Public Hearings: 
Following public review of the Draft RPEIR and preparation and certification of the Final PEIR, the proposed 
project will be reviewed by the City’s Planning Commission and City Council. The Planning Commission 
will vote to recommend approval or denial of the project and the City Council will be the final authority on 
whether the project will be approved. Notification of these public hearings will be provided in accordance 
with state law and the City’s public notification requirements.
